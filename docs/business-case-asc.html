Investment Memorandum & Strategic ROI Model

Mend for US Ambulatory Surgery Centers

A post-operative remote safety net translating post-op surveillance into elective volume growth. (Integrated Critique Edition)

01Executive Summary

Total joint replacement is shifting rapidly to the outpatient setting. While surgical and anesthetic protocols for same-day joints are highly mature, post-discharge observation has been cut from days to hours. An Ambulatory Surgery Center (ASC) discharging a patient 4 hours post-op lacks the safety infrastructure of an inpatient ward.

Mend is an automated, voice-first remote safety net. **Our core thesis is that Mend's primary commercial value is capacity expansion, not complication reduction.** By establishing a secure, clinically audited home safety net, Mend allows ASC medical directors to safely broaden patient selection criteria—admitting older, higher ASA class (ASA III), or socially isolated patients who would otherwise be referred to inpatient hospitals. Every additional patient accepted represents significant facility contribution margin.

The "Killer Slide" Metric: For a typical 400-case orthopaedic ASC, the annual cost of Mend is fully amortized by recovering just **12 cases per year** (a 3% increase in elective arthroplasty throughput).

02Industry Overview

Regulatory and financial alignment is accelerating the migration of major joint arthroplasty:

  • Total Certified ASCs: 6,120 (CMS Q4 2025).
  • Orthopaedic ASCs: ~1,480 centers where orthopaedics is the dominant or sole service line.
  • ASCs Performing Total Joints (TJA): ~820 centers. The capital expenditure for total joint setups (sterilizers, specialized instrument sets, overnight holding beds) restricts TJA to high-volume clinics.
  • Outpatient TJA Migration: Outpatient total joint replacement (TJA) grew from 9% in 2018 to 52% in 2025, and is projected to reach 74% by 2030.
  • Payer Mix & Medicare Advantage (MA) pressures: Typical TJA payer mix in ASCs is 35% Medicare / MA and 65% Commercial/Employer-sponsored plans. MA plans use tighter narrow-network boundaries. **Mend serves as the required home-safety net that enables ASCs to bid for high-acuity MA joint replacement contracts.**

03Customer Problem Analysis

An ASC Administrator's primary concerns are operational efficiency and financial margin, not clinical novelty. Mend solves their actual problems:

  • OR Utilisation & Throughput: Maximizes cases per room per day (target 5-6 joints/room) by moving deferred/hospital-bound patients into the ASC.
  • Surgeon Satisfaction: Minimizes manual phone work for the surgeon's clinical team.
  • Post-Discharge Liability: Provides a continuous, legally auditable digital record of post-operative vigilance.

05Market Failure Analysis

Why hasn't this opportunity been addressed by incumbents?

  • Epic/Cerner (EHR Giants): Assume the patient is tied to an enterprise health system. **92% of ASCs run lightweight, specialized EHRs** (e.g., HST Pathways, SIS) and are ignored by hospital-centric software. **Mend integrates directly with HST Pathways and SIS Link via scheduling API hooks to prevent double-entry and coordinator friction.**
  • Hinge Health / PT Apps: Focus on long-term exercise compliance and physical therapy replacement. They use smartphone apps that have low compliance among elderly patients.
  • Device Giants (Stryker/Zimmer): Software is locked to proprietary implants, failing the open-platform needs of multi-surgeon ASCs.

06Commercial Model & Procurement

ASCs are physician-owned entities. Procurement bypasses hospital committees, relying on three key actors:

  • Surgeon-Partners: Hold the majority equity. Approve Mend because it increases their facility distributions.
  • Medical Director: Owns patient safety. Approves Mend because it mitigates liability of higher-risk patients.
  • ASC Administrator: Manages operations. Approves Mend because it reclaims nurse hours and protects quality scores.

Mend is billed as an annual subscription paid from the ASC's Operating Expense (OpEx) budget, requiring no capital expenditure approvals.

Load-bearing Risk Warning

If you set the declined-patient count or the acceptance share to zero, the net case economics turn **negative**. Nursing efficiency and avoided hospital rescues alone do not justify a $95/case price point. Mend's commercial viability stands almost entirely on the **capacity expansion term**.

09Operational Strategy & Logistics

To eliminate deployment friction and nurse bottlenecks, Mend integrates a standardized operational strategy:

  • 1. Pre-Op Drop-Ship Loop: To avoid PACU congestion during discharge, Mend partners with a medical supply distributor to drop-ship the vital monitoring kit (cellular blood pressure cuff, pulse oximeter, thermometer) directly to the patient's home 7 days *before* their scheduled surgery.
    Double Benefit: Patients receive pre-operative setup training, and the clinical team collects 7 days of baseline physiological vitals to configure patient-specific thresholds.
  • 2. Asynchronous Triage & Clinical Alerts: Standard daily monitoring alerts are routed to an asynchronous nurse triage dashboard. Overnight (2:00 AM) red-line alerts are automatically routed to the ASC's existing on-call paging service or our outsourced clinical triage partner, ensuring zero nurse burnout during standard shifts.
  • 3. EHR-Agnostic with Integration Hook: Mend deploys out-of-the-box in 14 days without IT department bottlenecks. However, we build lightweight API syncs with scheduling databases (HST Pathways, SIS Link) to prevent double-entry of patient demographics.

12Validation Roadmap

Exactly what must be proven to achieve institutional-grade scaling:

  1. Month 1: Customer Discovery Interviews Interview 20 ASC medical directors and 10 administrators to verify declined-patient volumes and selection thresholds.
  2. Month 2: Retrospective Validation Run the safety engine against 200 historical patient files to calculate threshold specificity and sensitivity.
  3. Month 3: Clinical Pilot Deploy a 50-patient clinical pilot at a single surgeon partner's ASC to audit device compliance and verify nurse labor savings.
  4. Month 6: Economic Study Publish a cost-effectiveness study to support enterprise MSO sales.
