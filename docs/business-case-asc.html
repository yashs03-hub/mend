Strategic Analysis & ROI Model

Mend for US Ambulatory Surgery Centers

A clinical-grade safety net translating post-op surveillance into elective volume growth.

01Executive Summary

Total joint replacement is shifting rapidly to the outpatient setting. While surgical and anesthetic protocols for same-day joints are highly mature, post-discharge observation has been cut from days to hours. An Ambulatory Surgery Center (ASC) discharging a patient 4 hours post-op lacks the safety infrastructure of an inpatient ward.

Mend is an automated, voice-first remote safety net. **Our core thesis is that Mend's primary commercial value is capacity expansion, not complication reduction.** By establishing a secure, clinically audited home safety net, Mend allows ASC medical directors to safely broaden patient selection criteria—admitting older, higher ASA class, or socially isolated patients who would otherwise be referred to inpatient hospitals. This unlocks substantial facility contribution margins.

02Market Overview & Trends

Regulatory and financial alignment is accelerating the migration of major joint arthroplasty:

1. CMS Covered Procedures List (CPL): Medicare removed Total Knee Arthroplasty (CPT 27447) from the Inpatient-Only (IPO) list in 2018, followed by Total Hip Arthroplasty (CPT 27130) in 2020. Both are now fully covered in the ASC setting.

2. Payer Mandates: Commercial payers actively redirect cases to ASCs, where the total cost of a joint replacement is 30–40% lower than in a hospital outpatient department (HOPD).

3. Outpatient Velocity: Outpatient joint replacement volume in the US is projected to grow by over 100% between 2020 and 2030, representing the fastest-growing sector in elective orthopaedic surgery.

03Why Now?

The clinical problem has shifted from the operating room to the home. Serious post-op complications do not peak on the day of surgery. Venous thromboembolism (DVT/PE) peaks between days 7 and 10, while prosthetic joint infection (PJI) typically presents in weeks 2 to 4. ASCs currently rely on a single 24-hour post-op follow-up call, leaving patients unmonitored during this critical window. Unplanned hospital returns within 30 days are heavily scrutinized quality metrics that damage surgeon referral patterns.

04Clinical Problem & Mend's Safety Net

ASCs face a dilemma: maintain overly conservative selection criteria and decline profitable cases, or accept high-risk patients and face readmission penalties. Mend resolves this with a three-layer remote surveillance protocol:

* **Voice-First Assessment:** Automated daily telephone check-ins bypass smartphone apps, achieving >90% compliance in elderly joint cohorts.

* **Objective Vitals Fusion:** Fuses patient symptom reports with objective physiological data (heart rate, blood pressure, temperature) via connected home monitors.

* **Deterministic Safety Engine:** Evaluates check-ins using a transparent, audited ruleset (e.g., [red-flag-engine.ts](file:///Users/yashsewpaul/code/mend/lib/clinical/red-flag-engine.ts)) to prevent LLM hallucinations, outputting structured SBAR handoff summaries to clinicians.

Load-bearing Risk Warning

If you set the declined-patient count or the acceptance share to zero, the net case economics turn **negative**. Nursing efficiency and avoided hospital rescues alone do not justify a $95/case price point. Mend's commercial viability stands almost entirely on the **capacity expansion term**.

06Market Sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)

* **Total Addressable Market (TAM):** ~1.25M elective primary joint replacements (THA/TKA) performed annually in the US. At $95/case, this represents a **$118M TAM**.

* **Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM):** ~400k joint procedures shifting to Ambulatory Surgery Centers annually, generating a **$38M SAM**.

* **Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM):** ~60k joint cases performed across 150 single-specialty orthopaedic ASCs running high-volume total joint programs (SOM: **$5.7M**).

08Go-To-Market & Pricing Strategy

  1. Physician-Owned ASCs (Total Joint Programs) Initial GTM target. Surgeon-owners bypass hospital purchasing committees and pocket the facility contribution margin of every recovered case.
  2. ASC Management Corporations (MSOs) Partnership with operators managing hundreds of ASCs (e.g. USPI, AmSurg, SCA Health) to deploy Mend under shared clinical governance.
  3. At-Risk Orthopaedic Groups & conveners Selling directly to orthopaedic groups operating under bundled-payment contracts (e.g., CMS BPCI-A), where readmissions directly erode profitability.

Pricing: Volume-tiered annual subscription (e.g., $30,000/year for up to 350 cases). Bypasses case-by-case purchasing approvals, aligning with standard ASC operational software procurement. A center breaks even on the annual subscription by recovering just **12 additional cases per year**.

09Risks and Assumptions

High
Global Period Billing Constraints Post-op joint care within the 90-day global surgical period is bundled. Separate billing under RPM/RTM codes is contested. Mend mitigates this by clearing on **pure facility margin (capacity)** rather than reimbursement.
High
Clinical Safety Surface Area Direct patient advice carries liability. Mitigated by restricting patient-facing calls to directive triages (e.g., "call your surgeon's office") while generating detailed SBAR summaries for clinicians to review.
Medium
Compliance and Adherence Remote monitoring fails if patient equipment stays in the drawer. Mend resolves this by using the automated voice call itself as the prompt for the patient to record their vitals.
