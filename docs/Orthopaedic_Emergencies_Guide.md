# Orthobullets Clinical Guide: Orthopaedic Emergencies
*A comprehensive guide to critical orthopaedic conditions requiring immediate surgical evaluation and intervention to prevent limb loss, permanent neurological deficit, or death.*

---

## 1. Acute Compartment Syndrome (ACS)

### Description
Increased pressure within a closed osteofascial compartment leading to microvascular compromise, muscle ischemia, and nerve necrosis.

### Key Clinical Indicators (The "6 Ps")
1. **Pain out of proportion** to injury and **pain on passive stretch** of the compartment muscles (the earliest and most sensitive clinical signs).
2. **Paresthesia** (indicates early nerve ischemia in nerves traversing the compartment).
3. **Paralysis** (late finding, indicating muscle necrosis).
4. **Pulselessness / Pallor** (very late findings; compartment pressure rarely exceeds systolic pressure, so pulses are usually *present* even in active syndrome).
5. **Poikilothermia** (cold extremity).

### Diagnostic Thresholds
* **Intracompartment Pressure (ICP)**: Normal is 0–8 mmHg.
* **Delta Pressure ($\Delta P$)**: 
  $$\Delta P = \text{Diastolic Blood Pressure} - \text{ICP}$$
  * A **$\Delta P \le 30 \text{ mmHg}$** is diagnostic of acute compartment syndrome and is the absolute surgical threshold.

### Surgical Management
* **Emergency Fasciotomy**: Complete decompression of all compartments in the affected limb within **4–6 hours** of symptom onset to prevent irreversible contractures (e.g., Volkmann's ischemic contracture) or amputation.

## 2. Septic Arthritis

### Description
Bacterial invasion of the joint space resulting in rapid cartilage degradation due to proteolytic enzymes released by white blood cells and chondrocytes.

### Clinical Presentation
* Acute onset of joint pain, swelling, erythema, local warmth.
* Severe restriction of passive and active range of motion.
* Constitutional symptoms: Fever, chills, tachycardia.
* **Inability to bear weight** on the affected extremity.

### Diagnostic Thresholds (Arthrocentesis)
Joint aspiration is the gold standard diagnostic test:

| Test | Septic Arthritis Criteria | Inflammatory / Gout Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **WBC Count** | **$> 50,000 \text{ cells/}\mu\text{L}$** (often $> 100,000$) | $2,000 - 50,000 \text{ cells/}\mu\text{L}$ |
| **PMN %** | **$\ge 75\%$** polymorphonuclear cells | $< 75\%$ |
| **Gram Stain** | Positive in 50–70% of cases | Negative |
| **Lactate** | Elevated in synovial fluid | Normal to low |

### Pediatric Hip Septic Arthritis (Kocher Criteria)
Used to differentiate septic hip from transient synovitis in children:
1. **Inability to weight-bear** on the affected side.
2. **ESR $> 40 \text{ mm/hr}$**.
3. **Fever $> 38.5^\circ\text{C}$ ($101.3^\circ\text{F}$)**.
4. **WBC $> 12,000 \text{ cells/}\mu\text{L}$**.

* **Probability Matrix**:
  * 1/4 criteria met: $3\%$ risk of septic hip.
  * 2/4 criteria met: $40\%$ risk.
  * 3/4 criteria met: $93\%$ risk.
  * 4/4 criteria met: $99\%$ risk (mandates immediate surgical irrigation).

### Surgical Management
* **Emergent Surgical Debridement**: Arthroscopic vs. open arthrotomy joint irrigation/washout, followed by empirical IV antibiotics adjusted after culture results.

## 3. Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES)

### Description
Severe compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots ($L2-S4$) within the spinal canal, most commonly caused by a massive central disc herniation.

### Clinical Presentation
* **Saddle Anesthesia**: Sensory deficit in the perineum, buttocks, and inner thighs ($S3-S5$ dermatomes).
* **Bladder/Bowel Dysfunction**: Urinary retention with overflow incontinence (the most sensitive indicator, $> 90\%$ sensitivity).
* **Motor/Sensory Deficits**: Bilateral lower extremity weakness, bilateral radiculopathy (sciatica), loss of deep tendon reflexes (achilles, patellar).
* **Sexual Dysfunction**: Poor rectal tone / loss of bulbocavernosus reflex.

### Emergency Action
* **Immediate MRI**: Emergency lumbar spine MRI.
* **Emergent Decompression**: Surgical laminectomy and discectomy within **24–48 hours** of onset. Decompression beyond 48 hours is associated with permanent neurogenic bladder, bowel, and erectile dysfunction.

## 4. Open Fractures (Gustilo-Anderson Classification)

### Description
A fracture associated with a breach of the overlying skin and soft tissues, communicating directly with the external environment.

### Classification & Antibiotic Protocol

| Gustilo Type | Wound Characteristics | Soft Tissue Damage | Primary Antibiotic Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type I** | $< 1 \text{ cm}$ length | Clean wound, minimal damage | 1st Gen Cephalosporin (Ancef) |
| **Type II** | $1 - 10 \text{ cm}$ length | Moderate damage, no flap needed | 1st Gen Cephalosporin (Ancef) |
| **Type III** | $> 10 \text{ cm}$ length | High-energy, extensive damage | Cephalosporin + Aminoglycoside (Gentamicin) |
| **Type IIIa** | $> 10 \text{ cm}$ length | Adequate soft tissue coverage | Cephalosporin + Aminoglycoside |
| **Type IIIb** | $> 10 \text{ cm}$ length | Inadequate coverage (requires flap) | Cephalosporin + Aminoglycoside |
| **Type IIIc** | Any size wound | Associated vascular injury | Cephalosporin + Aminoglycoside + Vascular repair |

* **Special Exposures**: Add **Penicillin G** for organic/soil contamination (clostridium risk), and **fluoroquinolones** for freshwater/saltwater injuries (vibrio/aeromonas risk).

### Emergency Action
* Sterile dressing application in the ED (do not probe or manipulate).
* Tetanus toxoid booster.
* Emergency operative irrigation and debridement within **24 hours** of injury.

## 5. Knee Dislocation (Tibiofemoral Dislocation)

### Description
High-energy disruption of at least 3 out of 4 major knee ligaments (ACL, PCL, MCL, LCL), representing a major orthopedic threat.

### The Popliteal Artery Compromise
* The popliteal artery is tethered to the femur at the adductor hiatus and the tibia at the soleus line, making it highly susceptible to transection or intimal tear (up to **40% incidence** in knee dislocations).

### Vascular Triage Algorithm
* **Physical Exam**: Check distal pulses (dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial) and perform **Ankle-Brachial Index (ABS)**.
* **Triage Thresholds**:
  * **Hard Signs of Vascular Injury** (active bleeding, expanding hematoma, cold/pale leg, absent pulses) $\rightarrow$ **Emergent surgical exploration and vascular bypass in OR**.
  * **Asymmetric Pulses / ABI $< 0.90$** $\rightarrow$ **Emergent CT Angiogram** to rule out intimal tear or flap.
  * **Symmetric Pulses / ABI $\ge 0.90$** $\rightarrow$ Serial physical examinations every 2–4 hours for 24 hours.

### Emergency Action
* **Immediate Closed Reduction**: Perform traction-countertraction reduction immediately. Do not wait for X-rays if pulses are absent.
* **Warm Ischemia Time**: Vascular reconstruction must be achieved within **6 hours** of injury to prevent ischemic contracture or amputation.

## 6. Necrotizing Fasciitis & Gas Gangrene

### Description
Fulminant, rapidly progressive soft-tissue infection characterized by widespread necrosis of subcutaneous tissue and fascia.

### Clinical Presentation
* **Pain out of proportion** to physical skin exam findings.
* **Crepitus** on palpation (indicates gas-producing organisms, e.g., *Clostridium perfringens*).
* Hemorrhagic bullae, skin fluctuance, dishwater-like drainage.
* Rapid progression to septic shock, acidosis, and multi-organ failure.

### Diagnostic Support
* **LRINEC Score (Laboratory Risk Indicator for Necrotizing Fasciitis)**: Evaluates CRP, WBC, hemoglobin, sodium, creatinine, and glucose. A score **$\ge 6$** raises high suspicion.

### Emergency Action
* **Emergent Surgical Debridement**: Immediate, wide-margin surgical resection of all necrotic fascia and tissue (often requiring serial debridement).
* **Broad IV Antibiotics**: Triple-agent therapy (e.g., Vancomycin + Zosyn + Clindamycin; Clindamycin is critical to suppress bacterial toxin production).
